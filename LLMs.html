An overview of the architecture

Embeddings

An embedding is a solution to a fundamental problem: computers deal with numbers and we want to input words or data into computers to understand. Therefore we need to convert our data into a numeric representation that computers can understand.

Embedding is a learned representation of a word or data as a numeric such that it captures the meaning or properties of the data. It allows machines to understand concepts and relationships.

This will make more sense as we delve deeper into this!

One Hot Encoding

This is the simplest form of embedding. Say we wanted to convert [cat, dog, humans] into this embedding. We would represent cat as [1, 0, 0] and dog as [0, 1, 0] and human as [0, 0, 1].
This means the position of each word is orthogonal to each other - furthest apart from one another.
Ideally we want words that are similar close to one another. Dogs and Cats are semantically close compared to humans, as they are both animals. However with one hot encoding this relationship is not captured. One hot encoding also introduces the issue big dimentionality as we would need a new dimention for every word.
